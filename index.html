Why This Matters

Under the current system, New Mexico advances the clock by one hour for roughly eight months each year through Daylight Saving Time but returns to Standard Time for only four months. In practice, this makes Daylight Saving Time the default and Standard Time, our natural clock based more closely on solar time, the exception.

This also means that one side of the daylight debate, those who prefer later evening daylight, receives the benefit of a one-hour time shift for most of the year (8 out of 12 months). Meanwhile, those who prefer more morning daylight, including students, teachers, parents, commuters, and early-morning workers, experience Standard Time only during the shortest and darkest days of the year and for only four months (4 out of 12 months).

True fairness would suggest that if clocks are moved forward to favor additional evening daylight, they should also be moved backward at another time of year to provide additional morning daylight for those who prefer it. Instead, the current system shifts time in only one direction for most of the year, favoring one preference over another. We believe this approach is neither fair nor balanced for all New Mexicans.

Our Message:
Daylight Saving Time has become the default, while Standard Time has become the exception. We believe New Mexicans deserve one fair, consistent, year-round standard that works for everyone.

What We're Proposing

  • End the twice-yearly clock changes.
  • Adopt Permanent Mountain Standard Time.
  • Maintain one stable time standard year-round.
  • Create a fair and balanced system for all New Mexicans.
  • Align the clock more closely with natural solar time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are we proposing a new time zone?
No. New Mexico would remain in the Mountain Time Zone.

Are we proposing permanent Daylight Saving Time?
No. We support Permanent Mountain Standard Time.

Do other states already do this?
Yes. Arizona has remained on Mountain Standard Time year-round for decades.

Why not keep changing clocks?
Many New Mexicans believe the twice-yearly clock changes create unnecessary disruption to families, schools, businesses, and daily life.
